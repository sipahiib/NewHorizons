## 1. Preferred: fish abundance can conceal physiological stress

OIST, CNRS and Indiana University researchers sampled blue damselfish across 18 Okinawan coastal sites. Genome-wide expression revealed urbanization-associated immune/inflammatory activity alongside nutritional signatures resembling well-fed laboratory fish. Viewer relevance: counting visible animals alone may miss ecosystem damage; molecular monitoring could complement conventional surveys.

Primary release, dated **9 October 2026**: https://www.oist.jp/news-center/news/2026/10/9/fish-may-look-fine-urban-waters-their-genes-tell-different-story

Underlying paper, separately dated **9 October 2026** in the institution's Article Information: *Landscape transcriptomics reveals ecological constraints on fish under anthropogenic coastal change*, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77751-2 . Publisher page could not be retrieved; date verified through primary institutional metadata, not independently against publisher full text.

Limits: observational field associations, one species and island; measured environmental covariates cannot rule out unmeasured confounding. The “junk food effect” is the authors' proposed explanation, not a demonstrated diet mechanism. This does not establish population decline, human seafood risk, or a ready-to-deploy universal biomonitor.

Hooks:
- “These fish look healthy. Their genes tell a different story.”
- “A busy reef can hide a stressed ecosystem.”
- “What if fish could report the pollution we cannot see?”

## 2. Alternative: wristwatch sensor takes sweat chemistry into field work

Tokyo University of Science released this **9 October 2026**: https://www.tus.ac.jp/en/mediarelations/archive/20261009_9677.html . The underlying ACS Omega paper is older: **21 September 2026**, https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.6c04240 . Treat this as a fresh institutional release, not a new October paper.

A printed reference electrode, sodium/potassium electrodes, fabric sampling layer and wireless electronics recorded ion-responsive signals during approximately 2h45m of one construction worker's normal day. Practical relevance: a step toward unobtrusive physiological monitoring during outdoor work.

Limits: single-worker feasibility demonstration; quantitative sweat measurement still needs controlled validation. No demonstrated dehydration diagnosis, heatstroke prevention or clinical benefit. Mass production is prospective.

Hooks: “A wristwatch is learning to read sweat.” / “Could your sweat become a live safety signal?” / “This wearable measures chemistry while work continues.”
