## M1 — Heat-shield experiments riding aboard a disposal spacecraft

**Source/release:** NASA Ames, 9 October 2026: https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/ames/nasa-demonstrates-next-generation-heat-shield-technologies/

**Event/date distinction:** Twelve KREPE-3 capsules aboard Cygnus XL will test thermal protection materials, shapes and sensors during disposal re-entry. The spacecraft left the station on 9 October; NASA's departure report targets deorbit for **12 October**, still future on this cycle's research date. The capsule installation photograph is dated 7 October. Departure evidence: https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/spacestation/2026/10/09/cygnus-xl-cargo-spacecraft-released-from-station-by-canadarm2/

**Why/viewer relevance:** Re-entry destroys unwanted cargo, but can also supply flight data for future spacecraft protection. Explain heating, sacrificial material loss and why sensors complement ground testing. Describe selected printed, felt-like and deployable designs without claiming certification.

**Boundaries:** No successful re-entry, recovered results or crew qualification is established yet. Recheck the event status before production; changing a planned event to an observed result needs evidence and assessment of whether framing changes materially.

**Visual route:** Direct capsule and material photographs from the release; recorded station departure on NASA+: https://plus.nasa.gov/scheduled-video/nasas-northrop-grumman-commercial-resupply-services-24-departure/ . Actual ground-test archive: https://plus.nasa.gov/video/nasa-arc-jet-facility-history/ (28 August 2023, Johnson facility, not KREPE-3). NASA reuse route: https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/ ; check individual third-party credits, remove source music and do not imply endorsement. ## M2 — A watch that samples sweat chemistry during work

**Source/release:** Tokyo University of Science, Japan, 9 October 2026: https://www.tus.ac.jp/en/mediarelations/archive/20261009_9677.html . Institution gives paper publication as **21 September 2026**, ACS Omega, DOI https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.6c04240 . Treat as a new institutional report of older research, not a newly published paper. Publisher full text was not accessible in this research pass.

**What/who/why:** Isao Shitanda, Takuma Fujisawa and colleagues combine printed reference and ion-selective electrodes with sweat transport and wireless recording in a wristwatch-format prototype. Sodium/potassium-responsive signals were recorded from **one construction worker over 2 hours 45 minutes**, including ordinary work and breaks. This illustrates wearable chemical sensing beyond step counts.

**Boundaries:** Quantitative sweat analysis still requires controlled validation. One demonstration does not establish dehydration diagnosis, heatstroke prevention, clinical benefit or general reliability. Manufacturing affordability is prospective.

**Visual route:** Direct device/design still: https://www.tus.ac.jp/en/mediarelations/20261009_9677_01.png (Isao Shitanda; institutional image states creator credit required). Other paper figures require individual licence verification. User media correction: construction footage is excluded. Ordinary smartwatch closeups (Pexels17770565 and4211107) and laboratory context accompany actual institutional device photographs. Ordinary watches do not demonstrate sweat sensing; narration distinguishes them from the research prototype. Direct device motion is unverified. See current main_manifest.json and MEDIA_AUDIT.md for audited selections.

**Hooks**
1. A wristwatch is learning to read sweat.
2. Can a wristwatch track the chemistry of sweat?
3. This wearable measures chemistry while work continues.

## R1 — Could chip design constrain frontier AI training?

**Source/release:** UC Berkeley, 9 October 2026: https://news.berkeley.edu/2026/10/09/a-global-hardwired-pause-of-frontier-ai-training-is-feasible-says-new-report/ . Primary report: https://hardwired-pause.ai/ ; executive summary: https://hardwired-pause.ai/executive-summary . Report release is identified as that day by Berkeley.

**What/why:** Berkeley-led researchers analyse a hypothetical international pause of frontier training. Their proposal would halt production of training-capable chips, progressively retire existing stocks and substitute hardware designed for inference. Explain training versus running an existing model and why compute supply could become a governance mechanism.

## R2 — Fish abundance can hide physiological stress

**Source/release:** OIST, Japan, 9 October 2026: https://www.oist.jp/news-center/news/2026/10/9/fish-may-look-fine-urban-waters-their-genes-tell-different-story . Institutional metadata dates the Nature Communications paper 9 October: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77751-2 . Publisher full text was unavailable; paper date and findings are attributed to OIST.

**What/who/why:** OIST, CNRS and Indiana University researchers studied blue damselfish across 18 Okinawan coastal sites. Urbanisation was associated with immune/inflammatory gene activity and adult nutritional patterns. Counts alone may miss physiological responses; transcriptomics could complement ecological monitoring.

**Boundaries:** Observational associations in one species and island; not proof of a single pollution cause, population decline or unsafe seafood. The suggested food mechanism is a hypothesis. Say gene activity, not genetic mutation. Monitoring application remains prospective.

**Visual route:** Seven fresh segments, provisionally five study stills and two actual reef-context clips. Adult-fish image: https://www.eurekalert.org/multimedia/1155581 (Emma Gairin, CC BY metadata). OIST offers juvenile, fieldwork, coast and map assets, whose individual permissions must be checked before selection. Context motion option: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tropical_Fish_Banner_Fish_on_Coral_Reef.webm (CC BY 3.0; bannerfish, not study species; label reef context). Alternative fresh footage candidate: https://www.pexels.com/video/vibrant-coral-reef-with-school-of-fish-underwater-35480156/ ; verify recorded origin and species before acceptance. Do not portray other fish as the sampled damselfish. No generated underwater visuals.

**Hooks**
1. Plenty of fish does not always mean a healthy reef.
2. A busy reef can hide a stressed ecosystem.
3. What if fish could report the pollution we cannot see?
