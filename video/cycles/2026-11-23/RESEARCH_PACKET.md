## Main programme candidates

### M1 (recommended lead) — BepiColombo is captured by Mercury
- **Region / field:** Europe and Japan (ESA/JAXA); space.
- **What:** On 21 Nov 2026, after an eight-year cruise (launched 20 Oct 2018), the joined spacecraft stack fired its thrusters and was captured into an initial elliptical orbit around Mercury. The stack is ESA's Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) plus JAXA's Mio inside its sunshield.
- **Who:** ESA's ESOC control centre (Darmstadt) and JAXA.
- **Why it matters:** BepiColombo is only the second mission to orbit Mercury and the first with two orbiters. It will study the planet's magnetic field, interior, surface and polar ice.
- **Viewer relevance:** Explains why reaching the planet nearest the Sun takes eight years and nine flybys: a spacecraft has to *lose* energy to get there. It also shows how real missions handle anomalies.
- **Sources:** ESA arrival pages and operations updates on esa.int (published 21–22 Nov; event 21 Nov); Anadolu Agency report. Next steps per ESA: MPO and Mio separate 9–10 Dec, Mio's sunshield is released around 16 Dec, routine science starts around April 2027.
- **Limitations:** ESA reports that a **software issue made the braking burn shorter than planned**. The spacecraft is safe and the resulting orbit is still being assessed. Use ESA's exact wording. Do not claim the schedule is unaffected or that the final orbit is known. *Unverified:* the initial orbit of about 590 × 11,640 km, and the mission cost of about €1.6 bn (secondary sources only). Omit both unless confirmed on esa.int.
- **Visual candidates:** *Direct:* ESA ESOC control-room footage from arrival day (ESA Multimedia / ESA YouTube); M-CAM image sequences from earlier Mercury flybys. *Contextual or illustrative:* ESA spacecraft renders as stills only. Orbit-insertion animations count as illustrative, so they must not be used as story imagery under the production spec unless clearly limited. Some items carry an "ESA/ATG medialab" credit. *Usage:* ESA material is usually CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO, but check each item. JAXA items need checking against JAXA's own terms. Real footage looks sufficient for roughly 5 stills and 7 clips. This must be confirmed in the media audit.
- **Hooks:** (1) "After eight years and nine flybys, Mercury has finally caught a spacecraft." (2) "The hardest place to park in the Solar System is right next to the Sun." (3) "Arrival day at Mercury, and a braking burn that ended early."

### M2 (recommended second) — EDITH: the largest trial of AI in breast screening starts recruiting
- **Region / field:** UK; medicine and AI.
- **What:** On 23 Nov 2026, Addenbrooke's Hospital (Cambridge University Hospitals) became the first of about 30 NHS sites to recruit. Up to about 700,000 women aged 50–71 attending routine screening will be involved over three years.
- **Who:** Co-leads Prof Fiona Gilbert (Cambridge/Addenbrooke's) and Prof Sian Taylor-Phillips (Warwick). Funded by the NIHR.
- **Why it matters:** NHS mammograms are read by two specialists, and fewer than 1% show cancer. The trial tests whether AI can safely flag concerns or let a single human reader handle routine negative scans, which could ease radiologist shortages.
- **Viewer relevance:** Directly relevant to women of screening age and their families. Clinicians remain responsible for decisions.
- **Sources:** warwick.ac.uk, cuh.nhs.uk, cam.ac.uk and nihr.ac.uk releases (23 Nov). The funding was first announced in 2025, so the new development is the start of recruitment.
- **Limitations:** This is a trial launch with **no results yet**. Do not claim that AI detects cancer better. *Unverified:* the Health Secretary's visit. The name appeared only in low-quality aggregators; omit it unless confirmed on gov.uk or CUH.
- **Financial point:** £10 m in NIHR funding.
- **Visual candidates:** *Direct* (if released): CUH/Warwick launch photos and footage; check the CUH media page. *Contextual:* NHS mammography or breast-unit footage, identified as such in narration. Direct motion footage is uncertain, so a still-heavy mix (about 4 stills and 6 clips) may need contextual clinical footage.
- **Hooks:** (1) "700,000 women, and one question: can AI help spot cancer earlier?" (2) "Your next mammogram could get a second opinion from AI." (3) "The world's biggest test of AI in breast screening has begun."

### M3 (alternative second) — A Riemann Hypothesis proof built with Claude passes machine checks
- **Region / field:** US (Anthropic), with an international verification community; AI and mathematics.
- **What:** On 20 Nov, Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, released a Lean 4 library of about 17 million lines that claims a formal proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, built with Claude. On 21 Nov, a Lean FRO and Mathlib statement reported that it compiled cleanly, that the formal statement matches Mathlib's RH, that it uses only standard axioms, and that independent kernel checkers replayed it.
- **Why it matters:** RH is a 167-year-old Millennium Prize problem, and this would be the first major result whose full proof no human has read.
- **Sources:** alpo.ge (20 Nov); Lean FRO / Mathlib statement on github.io (21 Nov); the Guardian; MIT commentary.
- **Limitations:** Very high stakes. There has been **no peer review and no Clay Institute recognition**, and experts are still auditing the definitions. Debate about human understanding and credit continues. Frame it as a "formally verified claim". *Unverified:* the roughly 250,000-page prose translation and individual expert quotes.
- **Visual candidates:** Weak for the main programme: a public-domain Riemann portrait and 1859 manuscript, screen captures of the public Lean repository (check the licence), and zeta-function plots (Wikimedia, CC BY-SA). There is no real event footage.
- **Hooks:** (1) "A 167-year-old maths problem, possibly solved, in a proof no human can read." (2) "17 million lines, and a computer says it's correct." (3) "A computer says it's true. Mathematicians say: not so fast."
