## Main lead M1 — Flexible blades for tidal power

**What and why:** Edinburgh researchers and collaborators tested a model tidal turbine with a flexible rubber trailing edge. Their 11 November peer-reviewed [Nature paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11278-w) reports comparable energy yield over a simulated tidal cycle against a model of an actively pitch-controlled turbine, with fatigue-inducing high-frequency load fluctuations reduced by up to one-third. Tides are predictable; reducing stresses could eventually make equipment simpler to maintain. The study was received 10 November 2025 and accepted 7 October 2026; the publication is new, not necessarily the experiment.

**Limits:** Model-scale tank tests at Edinburgh's FloWave facility and a simulated tidal-cycle comparison, not a long-term full-scale ocean demonstration. The active-pitch comparison is modelled; lower maintenance and cost are potential consequences, not demonstrated savings or guaranteed household electricity benefits.

**Visual plan:** Direct candidate: test-turbine photograph in the [paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11278-w), and [research report with test image](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-11-shifting-rubber-blades-tidal-power.html). Contextual real motion: [Edinburgh laboratory footage of model morphing blades](https://voilab.eng.ed.ac.uk/media) from a different 2021 test, and [FloWave tank video](https://open.ed.ac.uk/flowave/); identify as contextual in narration. Look for additional actual experiment recordings and relevant real tidal turbines; availability and rights remain unverified. Paper is CC BY 4.0 except marked third-party material; confirm each asset. Direct test video is not yet identified, so feasibility against the adaptable 5-still/7-clip target is unresolved. Do not use simulated animations as main imagery.

**Hooks:** M1-A “Could a rubber blade make tidal power tougher?” M1-B “A tidal turbine blade bends under pressure—and that may be its strength.” M1-C “The sea is hard on turbines. A flexible blade could change that.”

## Main story M2 — Hubble's gravitational lenses and Roman

**What and why:** On 12 November, [NASA released a gallery](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasas-hubble-offers-cosmic-sampler-of-targets-for-roman/) of Hubble gravitational-lens images, some candidates for follow-up with the Roman telescope. Foreground mass bends and magnifies light from more distant galaxies; the images help explain how astronomers study distant galaxies and dark matter. Roman launched on 30 August 2026; the gallery is the current development, not a new launch or new dark-matter discovery.

**Limits:** These are Hubble observations and prospective Roman follow-ups; do not imply the images were taken by Roman, that all targets will be observed, or that dark matter has been directly photographed or explained. Observations span earlier dates.

**Visual plan:** Direct stills: the NASA gallery and individual credited Hubble lens images. Real contextual motion: [NASA Roman launch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9wq3VHsL_bE) and [NASA launch materials](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasas-dark-universe-seeking-nancy-grace-roman-space-telescope-launches/); seek real Hubble deployment and Roman assembly recordings. ## Independent Reel R1 — Future of AI: proofs for encrypted messaging

[Google DeepMind's announcement](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/) and [technical report](https://google-deepmind.github.io/code-verification/), 10 November, describe human-guided AI agents generating machine-checked proofs of specified security properties in a particular compiled implementation of the Signal protocol. A proof checker tests logical validity against formal specifications; this approach may make rigorous security checks more practical.

**Limits:** Company-reported research, not a guarantee that Signal or every app, device or version is secure. Proofs rely on specifications and assumptions, and human experts review specifications. No evidence here of a general deployment or independent replication.

## Independent Reel R2 — Planet Earth: Scotland's carbon-rich bogs

[NASA Earth Observatory's 12 November image feature](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/scotlands-flow-country/) revisits Scotland's Flow Country blanket bog and its carbon storage; the Landsat image was acquired 4 March 2024. [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1722) describes the peatland and its 2024 World Heritage inscription. Explain waterlogged peat's long-term carbon storage and the value of protecting intact bogs. This is a new educational image feature, not a newly discovered peatland or newly conferred designation.

**Limits:** Carbon accumulation is long-term; no new sequestration rate or immediate climate result is established by this image. Avoid attributing all peatland carbon statistics to this site.
