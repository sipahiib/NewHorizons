## M1 — When satellite navigation lies

**Lead recommendation.** ESA's [2 October field-campaign report](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Satellite_navigation/Jammertest_pumps_up_the_jam_in_Norway) describes Jammertest in Norway: deliberate interference tests, preliminary Celeste signal acquisition under contested conditions and updated EGNOS monitoring prototypes. Viewer relevance: dependable positioning and timing support transport, emergency services and infrastructure.

**Dates and limits:** the tests took place in September, not October. Galileo's authenticated-position demonstration occurred on 16 September and was [already reported on 17 September](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Satellite_navigation/Galileo/Galileo_s_first_civil_authenticated_position_fix_under_spoofing_conditions). The current story is the later campaign report; do not call that milestone newly achieved. Celeste results are preliminary; SAS is an upcoming service, not protection already available on every phone. Authentication does not defeat every kind of interference.

**Visual candidates:** contemporary photographs linked from the report show spoofed phones, receiver testing, a navigation van and a drone. The [ESA documentary](https://www.esa.int/esatv/Videos/2026/02/Jammertest_strengthening_satellite_navigation) and [six-minute B-roll](https://www.esa.int/esatv/Videos/2026/03/Jammertest_strengthening_satellite_navigation/B-Roll) offer real receiver, antenna, vehicle and field-work shots. They show September **2025**, requiring a persistent archival/context label. Selected ESA licences and video terms are recorded in MEDIA_AUDIT.md; the unlicensed Testnor image is excluded. Use current stills to establish the 2026 work, and historical recorded footage only to explain testing.

**English hooks:**

2. “Engineers attacked satellite navigation to learn how to protect it.”
3. “A blue dot on your phone is not always telling the truth.”

## M2 — More information from a single ECG lead

Leiden University Medical Center researchers [published on 2 October](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44325-026-00153-2) a study using explainable deep learning to predict left-ventricular function from ECGs in a post-heart-attack population. Reported AUC was 0.883 for the pretrained single-lead model and 0.897 for twelve leads. This suggests a possible route toward more accessible monitoring.

**Dates and limits:** accepted 19 September; historical acute-coronary-syndrome data cover 2018–2023. The filtered single-lead dataset contains 460 patients and 5,480 recordings, 77.6% male. AUC measures discrimination, not percent accuracy. Do not claim ordinary smartwatches now diagnose heart failure, replace imaging or improve outcomes. The paper uses real home Withings smartwatch recordings; algorithm evaluation is retrospective, single-centre and has no independent external prospective validation. The publisher's accepted article may receive further edits.

**Visual candidates:** paper/supplement ECG and model-result figures are prospective stills under the article's CC BY 4.0, subject to figure-specific credits. Real contextual motion candidates: [ECG examination](https://www.pexels.com/video/a-man-having-an-electrocardiogram-8460066/), [ECG monitor](https://www.pexels.com/video/an-electrocardiogram-machine-8311312/), [smartwatch interaction](https://www.pexels.com/video/a-man-using-his-smartwatch-8026528/), and clinician review. Watch pulse-monitor footage is context, never evidence of ECG capture or the studied model. Selected Pexels files and licence conditions are verified in MEDIA_AUDIT.md. Add relevant shots only when correspondence is verified.

**English hooks:**
1. “Could one ECG lead reveal more than your heart rhythm?”
2. “Researchers found clues about heart function in a single ECG lead.”
3. “A smaller heart recording could carry more information than expected.”

## R1 — Future of AI: clearer hospital handoffs?

A [2 October Stony Brook-led paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03320-y) compared AI and human discharge summaries for 60 internal-medicine stays of 7–21 days. Clinician reviewers preferred AI summaries in 95% of encounters; fewer annotated omissions drove much of the improvement. The practical issue is communication between hospital and follow-up care.

**Limits:** retrospective clinician assessment, not a trial of patient outcomes. Estimated harm measures did not differ significantly. Human review remains essential. Accepted 16 September; encounters occurred in 2023–2024. GPT-5.2 summaries were generated in March 2026. The clinician comparison was unblinded.

**Seven-segment candidate approach:** four real contextual clips ([doctor typing](https://www.pexels.com/video/doctor-using-a-computer-6997946/), [medical note writing](https://www.pexels.com/video/a-lady-doctor-writing-on-a-medical-record-5998403/), [chart review](https://www.pexels.com/video/doctor-reviewing-patient-chart-in-hospital-36656061/), [clinician discussion](https://www.pexels.com/video/doctors-talking-together-5453568/)) plus three prospective stills: [doctor using tablet](https://www.pexels.com/photo/a-doctor-using-a-tablet-5234511/), [team reviewing information](https://www.pexels.com/photo/doctors-standing-with-tablet-5452187/), [follow-up discussion](https://www.pexels.com/photo/a-doctor-showing-a-tablet-to-a-patient-4266943/). Usage and prior-output checks passed; the three photo downloads were abandoned after access denial, and three still frames from the same newly downloaded clips were selected instead. See MEDIA_AUDIT.md for the final seven segments. Do not copy article figures: its CC BY-NC-ND licence is unsuitable as an assumed basis for adapted/commercial visuals. ## R2 — The Planet Earth: a fire makes its own storm

NASA's [2 October INSPYRE report](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/natural-disasters/wildfires/nasa-campaign-explores-clouds-spawned-by-wildfires/) describes aircraft and ground measurements of fire-generated clouds over western North America. Pyrocumulonimbus can inject smoke high into the atmosphere. Better understanding may support firefighter warnings and climate modelling.

**Dates and limits:** fieldwork was in summer 2026; the highlighted Wildhorse encounter occurred on 26 August. Analysis is now being assembled. Do not claim a proven forecasting system or quantify a newly established climate effect.

**Seven-segment candidate approach:** the report provides relevant campaign stills of a fire cloud, Gulfstream, ER-2, instrument pod, ground team, airborne laboratory and Wildhorse plume. A still-led Reel is allowed; substitute actual campaign motion only if verified. [INSPYRE's source hub](https://espo.nasa.gov/inspyre) supports further media discovery. Audit each NASA/INSPYRE credit separately; third-party campaign credits do not automatically have NASA reuse terms. All selected media must be newly downloaded and pass prior-output reuse checks.

**English hooks:**
1. “A wildfire can build its own thunderstorm.”
2. “Scientists flew through the smoke of a fire-generated storm.”
3. “Some wildfire smoke reaches far above ordinary clouds.”
