## Main-video candidates

### M1 — Main / space / Europe: Juice uses Earth as a steering wheel

**What happened:** ESA says its Juice spacecraft passed Earth on **28 September**, changing direction by 20° and increasing its velocity by 3.5 km/s on its path to Jupiter. The spacecraft also captured Earth images and exercised instruments. **Why viewers care:** a gravity assist trades careful navigation for propellant, leaving more fuel for science around Jupiter’s icy moons. ESA’s [event report, 28 September](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Juice/Successful_Earth_flyby_improves_Juice_s_course_to_Jupiter) is the primary account. [JMC image and licence](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2026/09/Juice_images_Earth_during_Earth_flyby) and [NavCam image and licence](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2026/09/Juice_NavCam_image_of_Africa_during_Earth_flyby) are dated 28 September.

**Limits:** flyby success and camera snapshots are confirmed; instrument science products were still being assessed. The mission has not reached Jupiter or detected life. ESA lists its arrival in 2031. **Motion plan, eight segments:** direct Juice flight animation (1–2; [ESA Television mission library](https://www.esa.int/esatv/Missions/Juice), rights/technical check required); direct ESA tracking or operations footage if available (3); direct spacecraft stills with restrained camera movement (4–5, licence attribution required); contextual Earth-from-space footage (6); direct trajectory graphic (7, verify reuse terms); direct mission animation toward Jupiter (8). Still fallbacks: ESA JMC/NavCam photos and a clearly labelled trajectory diagram. Never present an artist animation as live footage. Final rights and clip sufficiency remain open.

**Possible English hooks:** “A spacecraft just borrowed speed from Earth.” / “Why did a Jupiter probe come back to Earth?” / “This Earth flyby saved fuel for Jupiter’s moons.”

### M2 — AI / United States: GPT-6.1 Sol enters the model lineup

**What happened:** OpenAI announced GPT-6.1 Sol on **29 September** and describes near-Astra performance at a lower API cost, with rollout in Work and Codex. **Why viewers care:** developers can compare quality, speed, and cost on their own tasks. Primary sources: [launch announcement, 29 September](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-1-sol/), [model documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6.1-sol), [system-card addendum, 29 September](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-1-sol/respecting-auto-review), and [Enterprise/Edu release notes, 29 September](https://help-lb.openai.com/en/articles/10128477-chatgpt-enterprise-and-edu-release-notes). Underlying event: release on 29 September.

**Limits:** performance comparison is the maker’s claim; practical gains vary by task and access tier. The system card identifies elevated cyber and bio/chemical capabilities and associated safeguards. Avoid presenting benchmarks as independent validation. **Motion plan, five segments:** direct official product UI capture (1–2, rights/access check); direct model documentation screen (3); contextual coding/computer-use footage (4); direct comparison graphic built from verified official data (5). Still fallback: documented model table and release note. Screen captures must not show personal data.

### M3 — Technology / Japan: hydro plants get a shared remote-control upgrade

**What happened:** Toshiba and TEPCO Renewable Power announced a **29 September** contract with Yamagata Prefecture to replace monitoring/control systems across **14 hydro plants**, linking Toshiba remote-control hardware to a software service. **Why viewers care:** the story illustrates how existing clean-energy infrastructure is being modernized for operators. Primary [joint announcement, 29 September](https://www.global.toshiba/jp/news/energy/2026/09/news-20260929-01.html); contract signed that day.

**Limits:** this is a contract and intended deployment, not measured reliability or energy gains. The source is in Japanese; translation of the technical description needs a second check before narration. **Motion plan, five segments:** direct Toshiba/TEPCO facility footage if offered (1, rights check); contextual Yamagata hydropower exterior (2–3); contextual control-room operation (4); labelled system-flow graphic based on the announcement (5). Still fallback: supplier imagery or public facility photos after rights review. Do not imply generic footage shows these specific 14 sites.

### M4 — Medicine / United States: an mRNA vaccine formulation tolerates heat in preclinical tests

**What happened:** MIT researchers report an AI-assisted formulation for lipid nanoparticle mRNA vaccines that retained activity after high-temperature storage and produced immune responses in animals. **Why viewers care:** if later validated for real products, greater heat tolerance could ease distribution. Primary [Nature Biotechnology paper, 28 September](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03331-w) and [MIT explanation, 28 September](https://news.mit.edu/2026/new-formulation-helps-rna-vaccines-withstand-high-temperatures-0928). Underlying paper and announcement: 28 September.

**Limits:** laboratory storage and animal immunogenicity, including rodents and non-human primates, do not establish human protection, shelf life of licensed products, or regulatory approval. The paper’s abstract reports solid-state formulations at 37°C for over two months; avoid calling all mRNA vaccines room-temperature stable. **Motion plan, five segments:** direct MIT lab/researcher footage if licensed (1); contextual nanoparticle lab work (2); paper’s mechanism graphic, subject to publisher rights (3); contextual vaccine logistics (4); clearly labelled temperature comparison graphic (5). Still fallback: MIT press images, whose CC BY-NC-ND terms require a usage decision and credit; they cannot simply be repurposed commercially.

### M5 — Planet Earth / United States: pressurized wind tunnel tests turbine controls

**What happened:** MIT-led researchers used a highly pressurized wind tunnel to mimic full-scale wind-flow conditions and test how turbine alignment and blade settings affect output. **Why viewers care:** more reliable lab tests could guide improvements to existing wind farms. Primary [MIT report, 28 September](https://news.mit.edu/2026/pressurized-experiments-could-help-wind-farms-generate-more-power-0928) and [PNAS Nexus paper, 7 September](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/advance-article/doi/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgag303/8786541). The new reporting is 28 September; the underlying paper predates the three-day window. The paper is CC BY 4.0, subject to attribution and any figure-specific third-party credit.

**Limits:** the projected annual revenue gain is a model estimate, not observed deployment savings; omit it to avoid an unnecessary financial claim. **Motion plan, five segments:** direct pressurized-tunnel footage if MIT supplies it (1–2); contextual turbines from ground and aerial angles (3–4); labelled airflow/rotor-control animation built from the paper (5). Still fallback: paper diagrams with rights review. Generic turbines must be identified as contextual.

## Independent Reel candidates

### R1 — Future of AI: a public study asks users what they want from AI

Anthropic launched an AI-led interview study on **29 September**, running to 6 October. Participants may choose whether their full interviews become public. [First-party announcement and FAQ](https://www.anthropic.com/research/your-thoughts-on-ai), 29 September. **Relevance:** people can see one way developers gather feedback, plus the privacy tradeoff of sharing personal testimony. **Limits:** it is a study launch, with no new findings yet; Claude users are not a representative public sample, and published responses will be self-selected. **Seven-clip plan:** direct announcement capture (1); contextual people using AI (2–3); abstract interview-interface demonstration made without real responses (4); direct FAQ screen (5); contextual privacy/consent interaction (6); clear opt-in/opt-out graphic (7). Rights and actual interface capture require audit. Still fallback: first-party page and a labelled consent diagram.

**Hooks:** “Would you let your AI interview go public?” / “An AI company wants to hear your story about AI.” / “Your AI feedback could become a public record.”

### R2 — The Planet Earth: Mediterranean countries launch a water initiative

At a **28 September** Rome meeting, Mediterranean ministers endorsed a declaration and launched the Rome Initiative on Water; the European Commission reported it on **29 September**. The initiative aims to develop investable water projects and regional cooperation. [European Commission account](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/eu-welcomes-joint-solutions-fight-water-challenges-med-2026-09-29_en), 29 September. **Relevance:** water resilience affects households, farming, and ecosystems. **Limits:** a framework and initiative are commitments, not delivered water savings or funded projects; avoid implying impact is already measured. **Seven-clip plan:** direct meeting footage if available (1); contextual Mediterranean coast and dry landscapes (2–3); contextual reservoir and irrigation (4–5); direct Commission/initiative document capture (6); labelled basin map or water-use graphic (7). All contextual clips need location and rights verification. Still fallback: official meeting photo and map with permissions checked.

**Hooks:** “Can the Mediterranean plan for less water?” / “A new water pact has a hard test ahead.” / “From farms to taps, the Mediterranean is rethinking water.”
