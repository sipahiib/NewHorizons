## Main story — Mars had at least three distinct water systems

NASA/JPL reports that Perseverance's Margin Unit in Jezero Crater is igneous rock altered during at least three water episodes: carbon-dioxide-rich groundwater, the crater lake, and later heated groundwater that left calcium sulfate and fluorite. The work changes a simpler shoreline-sediment interpretation and helps reconstruct early Mars habitability. Viewer value: it shows how rover instruments revise conclusions drawn from orbit.

- Source: [NASA/JPL article](https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/mars/nasa-discovery-reveals-complex-water-systems-on-early-mars/), published 21 Sep 2026; underlying paper published 21 Sep in *Communications Earth & Environment*; rover observations include 2023 Margin Unit data.
- Limits: mineral evidence records water-rock interaction, not life; the sequence is inferred but the episodes cannot yet be dated.
- Eight-segment visual plan: Mars/Jezero orientation; Margin Unit panorama; orbital carbonate map; rover traverse; coarse olivine; fractured silica-bearing rock; SuperCam laser/chemistry; three-stage water timeline. Direct NASA rover imagery/data first; contextual mission animation and labelled maps second; still fallback from NASA/JPL. Confirm individual asset terms after approval.
- Hook options: “Mars did not have one water story. This rock records at least three.” / “Perseverance found a hidden water history written inside volcanic rock.” / “Orbit suggested a lake shore. The rover found something far more complicated.”

## Support 3 — A dissolving battery powered ingestible devices in pigs

Researchers built a cellulose-supported, beeswax-coated battery using magnesium, molybdenum trioxide and a biodegradable electrolyte. In pigs it powered an RFID transmitter and gastric stimulation that increased ghrelin. Viewer value: safer power could widen temporary ingestible or implantable electronics.

- Sources: [peer-reviewed paper, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44286-026-00443-7) and [Nature report](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02987-3), both 21 Sep 2026.
- Limits: pig study, not a human treatment; degradation took months in simulated gastric fluid; “bioresorbable” does not establish clinical safety, benefit or regulatory approval.
- Five visuals: device macro; material layers; capsule/RFID test; pig-study schematic; dissolve timeline. Seek author/institution media after approval; use original diagrams and licensed contextual lab footage; paper figure only with confirmed permission.

## Support 4 — Arctic melt-season length has paused, but risk has not

A NASA-led analysis of satellite observations from 1979–2023 finds the melt season is about 40 days longer than in 1979, yet its average length stabilized around 2010 as clouds and year-to-year weather gained influence over thinner ice. Viewer value: a flat recent trend is not evidence that Arctic warming stopped.

- Sources: [NASA Earth Observatory](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/arctic-melt-season-length-levels-off/), published 21 Sep 2026; [study DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-026-03772-6), *Communications Earth & Environment* 7, 552.
- Limits: stabilization may be temporary; melt-season length is not the same as ice thickness or extent, and the Arctic remains much warmer with thinner ice.
- Five visuals: 1979–2023 timeline; 2000s map; 2010–23 map; clouds/energy balance; thin-ice variability. NASA downloadable maps are public-domain unless noted; supplement with rights-cleared satellite/ice footage.

## Independent Reel — The Planet Earth

**A ‘fire amoeba’ resets complex life's heat record.** NASA-supported researchers report *Incendiamoeba cascadensis* reproducing at 63°C and remaining active at 64°C; the study appeared in *Cell* on 22 Sep. Source: [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/planetary-science/nasa-funded-research-finds-complex-life-defying-record-heat/), 22 Sep 2026.
