## Main lead — Swift's final chapter (US, space)

**What and why:** On 2 November NASA said its Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory was expected to re-enter on 4 November, ending an unusually long career tracking gamma-ray bursts and other short-lived cosmic events. Launched in 2004 for a two-year prime mission, it helped coordinate follow-up observations. The story explains why rapid alerts matter to astronomy and why re-entry forecasts remain uncertain.

**Evidence:** [NASA update, 2 November](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/swift/2026/11/02/nasas-swift-observatory-to-re-enter-atmosphere/); re-entry is a prediction for 4 November, not an observed event. NASA estimated 03:30 EST ±11 hours and said surviving fragments would be confined to latitudes 20.6° south to 20.6° north; predictions can change. NASA estimated risk of harm to anyone at approximately 1 in 2,100, not an individual person's risk. Recheck latest NASA update before any script or render. [AP, 1 November](https://apnews.com/article/e890935df7f468048594286566a96357) reports an attempted rescue did not succeed; do not infer re-entry location.

## Main second — AI drafts hospital discharge summaries (US, health technology)

**What and why:** Stony Brook researchers compared AI drafts with clinician-written discharge summaries for 60 complex hospital stays. Physician reviewers preferred AI drafts in 95% of the cases. Better handover documents could help continuity when patients leave hospital, but this is a research evaluation, not proof of improved outcomes.

**Evidence:** [Stony Brook release, 2 November](https://news.stonybrook.edu/?p=266389); [journal paper, published 2 October 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03320-y) and [preprint posted 5 June 2026](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.03.26354858v1). The university announcement is new; underlying research predates the release. One hospital; 60 stays lasting 7–21 days; reviewers assessed paired summaries. Both approaches contained errors, with no statistically significant difference in estimated harm severity or likelihood. Actual time savings, patient outcomes and routine deployment were not demonstrated; physician verification remains essential. The publisher’s indexed page identifies 2 October as the publication date and confirms the sample; it is an accepted early version subject to revision. Full article access remains limited; avoid claims beyond the indexed abstract and university release.

**Visual approach:** Direct: university release's credited research table and researchers' portraits are identifiable still candidates, subject to rights and legibility; patient records are private. Context: appropriately licensed hospital corridors, clinicians at computers, and patient consultations, explicitly identified as illustrative context, never as the study or real patient records. ## The Planet Earth Reel — Madeira's marina hitchhikers (Portugal)

**What and why:** A [Scientific Reports paper published 2 November](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-75432-0) examined long-term settlement-plate monitoring in four Madeira marinas. Its abstract reports 71 recorded taxa and 27–30 non-indigenous taxa per marina, making up 45–48% of cumulative recorded fouling species per marina. This illustrates how monitoring harbours may detect changing marine communities. Research dataset covers 2013–2025, not new observations in November. Non-indigenous does not automatically mean invasive or harmful; a marina study cannot establish impacts throughout the Atlantic or prove each arrival's transport route. The publisher provides an accepted early version subject to editing; the full paper could not be reopened via the research tool, so verify methods, figure rights and exact denominators before narration.

**Hooks:** Earth-A “What is quietly hitchhiking into island marinas?” Earth-B “A decade of underwater monitoring reveals a changing marina community.” Earth-C “The hidden life on a harbour wall can tell us where species are moving.”

**Seven-segment proposal:** 1 Madeira marina real footage; 2 boat hull fouling; 3 marine settlement plates; 4 close-up fouling organisms; 5 related Madeira monitoring photo; 6 research sampling; 7 same subject's marina habitat. A [2022 Madeira study](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/10/5/611) contains directly relevant contextual photographs of a different experiment and methods, not this study; verify individual image licensing and credits.
