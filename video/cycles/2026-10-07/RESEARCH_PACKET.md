- **What/who:** NASA released a Webb view of NGC 7129 showing young stars, protostars and outflows. Explain what infrared imaging reveals through dusty star-forming regions.
- **Sources/dates:** [NASA release](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/nasas-webb-captures-commotion-from-nebulas-stellar-jets/), 6 October 2026; [NIRCam image/data](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/webb/ngc-7129-nircam-image/), release 6 October; observations 17 December 2025.

- **Limits:** Newly released image, not a live view of stars forming this week. Assigned colours represent infrared filters. No discovery of life or immediate Earth impact is claimed.
- **Hooks:** 1. **Recommended:** “Behind this cloud of dust, Webb reveals stars still being born.” 2. “These colours tell the story of a stellar nursery.” 3. “What can infrared light reveal that ordinary cameras miss?”

Real recorded archival telescope context: [NASA SVS beauty-shot b-roll](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/12461) and [cleanroom work](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/12896), labelled archive with original dates. ## M2 — SANDO plans around moving obstacles

- **What/who:** MIT publicised a trajectory planner that builds time-dependent safe corridors and replans on an onboard computer. Practical motivation: autonomous navigation through changing, unfamiliar surroundings.
- **Sources/dates:** [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2026/planning-system-ensures-robots-flight-path-will-remain-collision-free-1007), 7 October 2026; [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07599), initially 8 April 2026; [IEEE record linked by MIT](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11714300), publication metadata inaccessible. Frame as a new institutional report about earlier research, not an experiment conducted this week.
- **Viewer relevance:** Why avoiding an obstacle's current position is insufficient when it can move; possible future uses include inspections and disaster response.
- **Limits:** Guarantees depend on obstacle-velocity bounds and estimation-error assumptions. Controlled tests do not establish safety in every disaster. MIT reports 12 dynamic flights; the accessible preprint abstract reports ten. Omit exact hardware trial totals until versions are reconciled. Never call it universally crash-proof.
- **Hooks:** 1. **Recommended:** “How does a drone avoid an obstacle that hasn’t moved yet?” 2. “A safe route can become dangerous in seconds. Can a drone plan ahead?” 3. “MIT’s new flight planner makes room for moving obstacles.”
- **Visuals:** Actual lab-flight portions of the paper's [supplementary video](https://youtu.be/_T10DJiLQXg) and MIT's embedded video; researcher/drone photos. Separate hardware from source simulations; exclude simulations as main-story imagery. MIT photos specify CC BY-NC-ND, attribution and cropping only; this does not establish video reuse rights. Rights audit after approval must establish eligibility. If unavailable, use licensed recorded drone/lab context clearly identified as context; material substitution returns to approval. No external permission request is authorised.
