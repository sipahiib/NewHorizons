## Main — NASA batteries and RNA droplets

Title: NASA's Cold-Tolerant Batteries & RNA Droplets That Move

Description:
NASA reports small solid-state battery prototypes working at temperatures down to minus80°C, while a separate survival test reached colder conditions. Then, Heidelberg researchers show how mutations can change the structure and movement of synthetic RNA droplets. Both stories explain early research and its limits: the batteries are not flight-ready products, and the droplets are laboratory cell models rather than newly created life.

NASA is first; RNA is second. Announcement dates14and13October2026 respectively; RNA paper published7October. NASA facility B-roll is contextual, not the SPEARS battery experiment. RNA footage is experimental time-lapse microscopy.

## R1 — AI storm simulations

Title: AI Makes Detailed Storm Simulations Cheaper

Description:
Ai2 introduces HiRO-ACEv2, combining AI climate emulation with three-kilometre rain, wind and pressure fields. The roughly300-fold inference-computation saving is the researchers' benchmark against X-SHiELD, not a claim about every weather model. This preprint does not prove better daily forecasts, and the current system cannot yet produce these detailed outputs for warmer future climates. Images show simulated fields, not recorded storms.

Sources: [Ai2 announcement14October2026](https://allenai.org/blog/hiro-acev2), [preprint13October2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17693).

## R2 — Hurricane Polo attribution

Title: What Strengthened Hurricane Polo? Warming and El Niño

Description:
A World Weather Attribution analysis released15October2026 investigates human-caused warming and El Niño in September's Hurricane Polo. The researchers find warming influenced storm intensity at least as strongly. They could not quantify the climate-change contribution to rainfall because models did not reproduce observed rainfall trends. This is a new attribution analysis, not a current hurricane warning.

Source: [World Weather Attribution](https://www.worldweatherattribution.org/effect-of-climate-change-at-least-as-large-as-el-nino-in-strengthening-hurricane-polo/).
