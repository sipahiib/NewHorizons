## M1 — RNA droplets gain movement and cargo compartments

**What / who:** Heidelberg, DKFZ and Augsburg researchers altered the DNA templates producing RNA droplets. Mutations produced stable internal cavities, movement on a microscopic scale and cargo storage. **Why / viewer relevance:** a tangible way to explain how genetic changes can yield new physical functions, helping audiences understand synthetic biology and origin-of-life experiments.

**Primary sources / dates:** [Heidelberg announcement](https://www.uni-heidelberg.de/en/newsroom/synthetic-cells-function-through-mutation), 13 October; [peer-reviewed paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44286-026-00454-4), published 7 October 2026. The announcement is inside the window; the paper is older. Experiment dates are not specified as a single event.

**Limits:** laboratory protocell models, not a newly created living organism or a demonstrated medical treatment. Avoid claiming autonomous Darwinian evolution, replication or targeted swimming. The templates were mutated experimentally.

**Visual route:** actual experimental microscopy in the paper's Supplementary Videos 1–4 and 6: control droplets, mutant droplets, cavity fusion and movement. Exclude Video 5 (theoretical animation). Plan five clean microscopy stills from the press image and experimental figure panels, plus seven non-overlapping experimental motion excerpts, subject to resolution, duration and embedded-label inspection. Narration identifies time-lapse microscopy. Paper licence is CC BY 4.0, with third-party exceptions checked per asset; retain author credit, licence and crop/speed-change disclosures in credits/descriptions. Static crops must preserve scientific meaning. If five video files do not provide seven useful excerpts, adjust the adaptable still/motion count without padding or inventing motion.

**Hooks:**
- M1-1: “A few genetic changes made these RNA droplets move.”
- M1-2: “These tiny droplets gained new functions after mutation.”
- M1-3: “Inside these RNA droplets, mutations built something unexpected.”

## M2 — NASA batteries tolerate extreme cold

**What / who:** NASA's SPEARS team reports small solid-electrolyte prototype cells operating down to −80°C, with reduced performance at that lower endpoint. **Why / viewer relevance:** explains why replacing a liquid electrolyte could help power equipment in harsh environments; terrestrial applications remain possibilities.

**Primary source / dates:** [NASA Langley report](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/langley/nasa-develops-batteries-to-power-humanitys-return-to-moon/), 14 October 2026. Thermal-vacuum tests described as recent; exact dates and a linked peer-reviewed performance dataset are not supplied.

**Limits:** distinguish operation from survival: exposure down to −170°C did not damage the cells, but they require warming before operation at such cold temperatures. Do not say they operated at lunar nighttime temperatures, are flight-qualified, eliminate heaters, or are commercially available. Capacity, cycle-life and scale-up evidence are insufficient here for consumer-product comparisons.

**Visual route:** four actual SPEARS photographs on the NASA report. No direct SPEARS video verified. Six contextual real-motion excerpts can come from [Space Environment Simulator B-roll](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/11807) and [STORIE TVAC B-roll Parts 1–2](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/14874), showing chamber hardware, preparation and testing operations. Narration explicitly introduces these as examples of NASA thermal-vacuum facilities, not the SPEARS experiment. No simulation or lunar concept animation. NASA credits and [media conditions](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/) apply; check each asset for third-party rights, avoid endorsement, omit source music and inspect clean frames. Counts are planning targets; exact cuts follow approval and audit.

**Hooks:**
- M2-1: “NASA tested tiny batteries at minus eighty degrees Celsius.”
- M2-2: “For Moon missions, surviving the cold is only half the battery problem.”
- M2-3: “A solid electrolyte could help batteries face the Moon's cold.”

## R1 — Future of AI: cheaper detailed storm simulations

**What / who:** Ai2 introduces HiRO-ACEv2, combining coarse AI climate emulation with downscaling to 3 km rain, wind and pressure fields. **Why / viewer relevance:** lower computation could let researchers examine more extreme-weather scenarios.

**Primary sources / dates:** [Ai2 announcement](https://allenai.org/blog/hiro-acev2), 14 October; [technical preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17693), submitted 13 October 2026. Training and simulated years are not new observations. The reported roughly 300-fold inference-cost saving is against X-SHiELD in the authors' benchmark, not every weather model or total development cost.

**Limits:** preprint and author evaluation against a reference simulation; not proof of better operational forecasts. Current system cannot yet generate the claimed high-resolution outputs for warmer future climates or arbitrary historical datasets.

**Seven-segment route:** seven sourced still panels: Figure 10 precipitation/wind/pressure; Figure 11 wind/pressure/precipitation; one relevant mountain-wind panel from Figure 5, in the [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17693v1). These are scientific simulation outputs, identified verbally, never recorded storms. Optional motion candidate: a faithful recording of the [official demo](https://ace.allen.ai/) if clean, useful and permitted; replace a still rather than adding an eighth segment. Paper is CC BY 4.0; blog/demo reproduction conditions need separate verification, and Apache-2.0 model licensing does not establish website-image rights. Use clean faithful map crops, no invented charts, no generic AI footage. If usable panels cannot meet clarity and rights requirements, return the visual approach to approval.

**Hooks:**
- R1-1: “AI is making detailed storm simulations cheaper to run.”
- R1-2: “This AI turns coarse climate output into finer storm detail.”
- R1-3: “Researchers can now simulate detailed storms for a fraction of the computing cost.”

## R2 — The Planet Earth: what strengthened Hurricane Polo?

**What / who:** World Weather Attribution's international team finds human-caused warming influenced Polo's intensity at least as strongly as El Niño. **Why / viewer relevance:** explains the distinction between storm strength, rainfall and local exposure, and why preparedness must include inland flooding.

**Primary source / dates:** [WWA analysis and full-study link](https://www.worldweatherattribution.org/effect-of-climate-change-at-least-as-large-as-el-nino-in-strengthening-hurricane-polo/), 15 October 2026. Storm occurred in September; this is a new attribution analysis, not a new hurricane alert.

**Limits:** rapid attribution, not a deterministic explanation of every impact. The team could not quantify climate change's rainfall contribution because models failed to reproduce observed trends. Potential-intensity likelihood ratios are not storm-occurrence probabilities; omit them from this short Reel. Do not describe preliminary analysis as an independently replicated result.

**Seven-segment route:** three distinct stills from [MODIS 23 September](https://modis.gsfc.nasa.gov/gallery/individual.php?db_date=2026-09-23) and [NASA Earth Observatory's Polo page](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/explosive-intensification-for-hurricane-polo/), plus four non-overlapping sections of [NASA/JAXA GPM/IMERG visualization](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5681/). Use the version without date overlays; distinguish satellite images from satellite-derived rainfall visualization in speech. No disaster stock. NASA/SVS and NASA/JAXA credits required; check third-party exceptions and clean frames. This mix is provisional pending native asset inspection; avoid implying rainfall visuals prove a quantified rainfall attribution.

**Hooks:**
- R2-1: “El Niño was not the only force strengthening Hurricane Polo.”
- R2-2: “A new study separates warming's influence from El Niño in Hurricane Polo.”
- R2-3: “What made Hurricane Polo so strong? Scientists have new evidence.”
