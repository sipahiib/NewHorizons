Slow motion does more than make sport look dramatic. It reveals the physics an athlete controls in milliseconds. A sprinter pushes backward against the track, and the ground pushes the body forward. A longer, stronger impulse changes momentum. Balance keeps that force travelling through the body instead of leaking sideways. The same ideas shape a jump, a football touch, or a basketball arc. Biomechanics turns motion into measurable forces — and shows that elite technique is applied physics.
