### Reel R1 — Sports Biomechanics and Physics: Joint Loading When Changing Direction After Knee Surgery

Approved replacement topic: Wiley's journal listing gives First Published 4 September 2026 for “Movement Strategies During the Penultimate and Final Steps of a Change of Direction Following Arthroscopic Partial Meniscectomy,” DOI 10.1111/sms.70367. Primary full-text retrieval remains pending; the publisher date and institutional author record were checked, while findings below derive from the authors' reproduced abstract.

Angle: the step before a turn helps distribute braking and propulsion across joints.
What happened / who: Starbuck and colleagues compared 24 participants after partial meniscectomy with 20 healthy participants during 90-degree direction changes, using motion capture and force plates.
Why engaging: apparently similar turns can involve different contributions from the ankle, knee and hip.
Practical lesson: researchers examine multiple joints and the approach step, not just the knee at the instant of turning.
Evidence boundary: the abstract reports ankle-dominant braking and hip-dominant propulsion in surgical limbs. Group-level laboratory evidence does not prove surgery caused the strategy, predict individual injury, or justify a self-directed rehabilitation exercise. Direct primary-text confirmation is required before final narration.
