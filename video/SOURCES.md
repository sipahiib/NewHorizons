## Proposed Main Story M1 — FAST Publishes a Catalogue of 156,411 Hydrogen Sources

Region: China, Far East.

Announcement date: National Astronomical Observatories of China, 8 September 2026; English news summary, 9 September 2026. The observing campaign ran from August 2020 through July 2025; the preprint predates this window. This is a new data-release story, not a newly built telescope.

What happened: the FAST All Sky H I Survey released its second data set, cataloguing 156,411 extragalactic neutral-hydrogen sources across about 19,500 square degrees.

Who/source: the FAST survey team at the National Astronomical Observatories, Chinese Academy of Sciences.

Why it matters: neutral hydrogen is the raw material from which galaxies form stars. A large open catalogue gives astronomers a new reference for studying galaxy gas, nearby large-scale structure and how galaxies evolve.

Viewer relevance: the immediate benefit is scientific infrastructure rather than a consumer product. Open survey data can let research groups study the nearby universe without repeating the full observation campaign.

Boundaries: this is a catalogue release, not proof of a new cosmological theory. It mainly covers the low-redshift nearby universe. Do not describe every catalogue entry as a newly discovered galaxy or every recorded radio signal as a message.

Factual sources:

- [National Astronomical Observatories of China — FASHI DR2 release, 8 September](https://www.nao.cas.cn/news/gd/202609/t20260908_8279679.html)
- [FASHI DR2 technical and data page](https://zcp521.github.io/fashi.html)

Region: United States. UC Berkeley announcement: 9 September 2026; peer-reviewed Nature publication: 9 September 2026.

What happened / who: UC Berkeley researchers reported GPN-Star, an AI model trained across species and individuals to predict how changes in human DNA may affect biological function.

Why it matters: much of the genome remains difficult to interpret, especially outside protein-coding regions. Evolutionary patterns can help researchers prioritise variants for closer study.

## Support M5 — Twin Sentinel-6 Satellites Measure Ocean Heat Linked to Hurricane Strength

Region: United States and Europe. NASA/JPL announcement: 9 September 2026. Sentinel-6B began supplying low-latency data on 15 July 2026; the timely story is the explanation of how the two-satellite configuration is being used during the current El Niño.

What happened / who: NASA, NOAA and European Copernicus partners reported that Sentinel-6B is flying 30 seconds behind Sentinel-6 Michael Freilich while both measure sea-surface height, waves and marine wind speed. Ocean height helps estimate stored ocean heat, one ingredient used in hurricane-intensity forecasting.

Why it matters: tropical cyclones can intensify rapidly before landfall. Consistent ocean-height measurements can improve the data feeding forecast research and operational algorithms.

Viewer relevance: better ocean observations can support earlier, better-informed emergency planning for coastal communities, but the new data still need to be incorporated into models.

Boundaries: this is not proof that Sentinel-6B has already improved a particular forecast or prevented losses. Satellites do not predict hurricanes alone, and El Niño shifts probabilities rather than determining any single storm.

Factual sources:

- [NASA/JPL — How two US-European satellites are studying hurricanes during El Niño, 9 September](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/jason-cs-sentinel-6/how-2-us-european-satellites-are-studying-hurricanes-during-el-nino/)

## Reel R1 — Does Running Form Change From Fatigue or Speed?

Category: Sports Biomechanics and Physics. Freshness: near-window; Version of Record online 13 August 2026 and assigned to a September issue, not a 7–10 September announcement.

What/who: Hunter, Karsten, Burnley, Greenhalgh and Muniz-Pumares tested 21 male runners on a treadmill at three intensities with 3D motion analysis. Speed/intensity-related changes were more consistent than changes accumulating over time.

Why/viewer relevance: visible gait changes during a harder run should not automatically be called fatigue.

Boundaries: small male-only lab sample; treadmill and sequential intensity order; speed and metabolic demand were not fully separable. The metrics are not an injury diagnosis or coaching prescription.

Sources: [PubMed Central full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13472544/), [publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ejsc.70236), [Greenwich repository](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/54029/).

## Reel R2 — Earth Reflects Sunlight With a Surprising East–West Balance

Category: The Planet Earth. Freshness: peer-reviewed 2026 result announced 3 June 2026; selected as a durable explanatory Reel rather than a 7–10 September news claim.

What/who: NOAA/CIRES researchers analysed a 25-year satellite record and found that a line near 27° east divides Earth into eastern and western halves that reflect nearly equal total sunlight, despite different cloud regimes.

Why/viewer relevance: the balance offers a new benchmark for testing whether climate models reproduce interactions among clouds, clear skies, oceans and ice. None of the eight models examined reproduced the full three-part symmetry.

Boundaries: the balance is an observed long-term pattern, not proof that both halves have identical climates or that climate change has stopped. The authors report that the east–west imbalance trend was not statistically significant over the record; future behaviour remains uncertain.

Sources: [NOAA Chemical Sciences Laboratory summary](https://csl.noaa.gov/news/2026/444_0603.html), [Nature paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10624-2).

Hooks:

- R2-H1 (recommended): “Earth’s eastern and western halves reflect almost the same total sunlight.”
- R2-H2: “Different clouds, different oceans—yet Earth balances reflected sunlight east to west.”
- R2-H3: “A hidden line near eastern Africa splits Earth into two equally bright halves.”
