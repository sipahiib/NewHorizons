Earth's eastern and western halves reflect almost the same total sunlight.

NOAA and CIRES researchers found this balance in twenty-five years of satellite measurements, using a dividing line near twenty-seven degrees east.

The two halves achieve it differently. Clouds, clear skies, oceans and ice contribute in different combinations, yet the total nearly matches.

That gives climate models a demanding test. In this study, none of eight models captured the complete pattern.

This does not mean east and west have identical climates, or that climate change has stopped. It is a long-term planetary balance whose future remains uncertain.
