A more acidic ocean shrank squid brains by nearly half in a controlled study.

Researchers kept bigfin reef squid for ninety days in present-day seawater or conditions designed to simulate ocean acidification in 2100. Academia Sinica reports that the acidified group had forty-nine percent lower average total brain volume. Their optic lobes were fifty-two percent smaller, and the optic tract sixty-two percent smaller.

Body size showed no clear difference, and earlier results found basic visual responses remained, although hunting performance and neural metabolism changed.

This was one species in a controlled scenario. It does not prove wild squid brains have already shrunk or that every marine animal will respond the same way.
