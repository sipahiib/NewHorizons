A temperature sensor may also be involved in chronic pain. Warwick researchers studied TRPM2, an ion channel that detects mild warmth, and found it can convert inflammatory and immune signals into pain-related electrical activity.

Removing TRPM2 from sensory nerves reduced pain behaviours in mouse models. In the arthritis experiment, blocking it with a joint injection reversed pain behaviour for two days without suppressing inflammation.

Instead of broadly reducing inflammation, a future therapy might target how sensory nerves translate inflammatory signals into pain.

But the result comes from mice and cells. It does not demonstrate a safe dose, effective human treatment or arthritis cure. Side effects and lasting benefit require extensive testing. TRPM2 is a possible target and a clue to pain signalling—not a medicine patients can receive.
