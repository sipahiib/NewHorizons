Ultrathin electronics require different contacts for different transistor types. A KAIST-led team reports one material that can work with both.

Contacts are a major challenge because ordinary metal deposition can damage extremely thin semiconductor layers or create resistance that wastes energy.

The researchers used a tin-diselenide van der Waals injector with p-type tungsten diselenide and n-type molybdenum disulfide devices.

They also demonstrated a CMOS inverter. KAIST says the p-type device reached a maximum drive current more than one thousand times its nickel comparison.

A shared contact approach could eventually simplify circuits built from atomically thin materials, helping engineers explore smaller, lower-power electronics.

These are laboratory devices, not a new commercial processor. Large-area growth, manufacturing yield, integration and durability still need work, and the reported performance does not apply to every chip.
