Korean manufacturers are starting AI plans with factory-floor problems.

At a government-backed hackathon, around one hundred participants from manufacturing, artificial-intelligence companies and related organisations developed possible projects for regional industrial sites.

Proposals included predictive maintenance, computer-vision inspection, capturing workers’ know-how as training data, and process simulations. Teams also examined which process to target, what data and investment would be needed, and whether a project could move into a supported demonstration.

That offers a practical lesson: successful factory AI begins with a measurable bottleneck, reliable data and a deployment plan—not with adding AI everywhere.

These were proposals and examples, not independently measured productivity gains. Follow-up demonstrations must still show whether the systems work safely and economically in real factories.
