A robot can reach the wrong place and still believe its journey is finished. A team is teaching navigation software to check again.

KAIST and ETRI's CoRe-VLN system rescans its surroundings when it thinks it has arrived. If the destination does not match, it replans the route.

It addresses a failure mode where a robot stops at a visually similar but incorrect place.

KAIST reports a ninety-point-seven percent average across two benchmark tasks. The system ranked first among one hundred and twelve teams, with a tie decided by earlier submission.

Destination verification could eventually make delivery, hospital and factory robots less brittle when layouts, viewpoints or instructions create uncertainty.

But this is a competition benchmark, not a production deployment or proof of universal safety. Real buildings bring people, moving obstacles and rare situations that benchmarks cannot fully represent.
