Two satellites are flying over the same stretches of ocean just thirty seconds apart.

Sentinel Six Michael Freilich and Sentinel Six B are a joint mission of NASA, NOAA and European partners. They measure sea-surface height, waves and marine wind speed.

Warmer water expands, so ocean height can help researchers estimate where heat is stored below the surface. That matters for hurricanes because unusually warm, deep water can support rapid intensification.

The twin arrangement lets scientists compare measurements almost immediately and check the consistency of the long climate record.

For coastal communities, better observations could strengthen forecasting and emergency planning. But satellites do not predict hurricanes alone. Their measurements must be combined with atmospheric observations and models, and NASA has not claimed Sentinel Six B has already improved a particular forecast. Integration supports better warnings.
