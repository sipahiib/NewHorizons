Asteroid Ryugu samples revealed an under-recognised part of their chemistry. A Japanese collaboration used synchrotron X-rays and solubility experiments to identify poorly crystalline, magnesium-rich hydrous silicate resembling magnesium silicate hydrate, or M-S-H.

The material appears in Hayabusa2’s Ryugu samples and in carbonaceous meteorites. It may explain how these primitive materials were altered by water while preserving early Solar System chemical differences.

This is not evidence of oceans on Ryugu, but a record of water interacting with rock in its parent body.

The phrase “invisible matter” needs care. It does not mean dark matter, and this is not evidence of life. It describes a mineral component earlier methods had not adequately recognised. Returned samples let laboratories revisit ancient material with new techniques and uncover previously hidden chemistry.
