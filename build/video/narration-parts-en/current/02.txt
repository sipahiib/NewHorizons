NASA has switched on the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope's main camera and successfully communicated with it during commissioning.

The Wide Field Instrument has three hundred megapixels. Between September eleventh and thirteenth, engineers recorded deliberately out-of-focus starlight as an initial baseline.

Its field of view is at least one hundred times larger than Hubble's infrared camera.

That blur was expected, not a failure. NASA also said communication with the coronagraph, plus thermal and mechanical checks, proceeded as planned.

Roman is designed to survey large areas of sky efficiently, helping scientists study cosmic expansion, the distribution of matter and worlds around other stars.

But this was an instrument checkout, not a discovery. Commissioning continues, and first science images are expected in 2027. Roman has not yet mapped dark energy or directly imaged an exoplanet.
