In medicine, MIT-led researchers developed a patient-specific artificial-intelligence system called x-v-r. It matches two-dimensional X-rays taken during a procedure with a patient’s three-dimensional CT or MRI scan prepared beforehand.

The team reports that the system can adapt to one patient in about five minutes, then register new X-ray images in seconds with sub-millimetre precision. It was evaluated using real data from five hospitals, including adult and paediatric cases.

That alignment could eventually give clinicians more reliable spatial guidance during minimally invasive procedures, connecting a limited X-ray view with richer information in the earlier scan.

But this remains a research system, not an autonomous surgeon. The study does not show improved patient outcomes or establish clinical approval. Moving anatomy and broader conditions still need testing. The promise is future guidance—not treatment available today.
