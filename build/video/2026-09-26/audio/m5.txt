Deep in the Swiss Alps, researchers have completed another controlled experiment on how faults move. During FEAR Three at the Bedretto laboratory, water was injected into boreholes while instruments monitored the surrounding rock. The operations ran from September twenty-first to twenty-third. The team reports a local-magnitude zero point six event before injection was stopped, with monitoring continuing afterwards. The purpose is to collect detailed measurements close to an activated fault. Those observations can help researchers test ideas about how movement begins and develops. The laboratory reports that shaking outside the tunnel remained very low. Still, this is an early account of an experiment whose data now need analysis. It is not a demonstration that scientists can predict or prevent damaging earthquakes. Its value is the opportunity to study fault behaviour in a closely monitored setting.
