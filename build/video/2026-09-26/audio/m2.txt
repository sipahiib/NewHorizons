Meta wants its personal AI agent to work through the glasses you wear. At Connect, the company announced plans to bring Muse to its AI glasses, alongside more connections to everyday services. The idea is to turn something you are looking at into a task. A product on a shelf or a list in front of you could become information the agent uses, without a long spoken description. Meta also describes voice conversations that continue while work happens in the background. That could make an assistant easier to reach. But a staged demonstration does not establish everyday accuracy or reliability. These are company announcements, with features arriving on different schedules. They should not be read as worldwide availability today. The useful question is what the agent can complete correctly, and how much access you choose to give it. Clear controls and visible task histories will matter as these systems develop.
